**1/**
A temple flag snaps in the wind. Two monks are arguing about it. One says the flag is moving. The other says the wind is moving. Neither will give ground.

A man steps between them: "It isn't the wind moving," he says. "It isn't the flag moving. What's moving is your mind." 🧵👇

---

**2/**
The man was Huineng¹, the Sixth Patriarch, lodging unrecognized in a temple's outer corridors. He'd been hiding for years after receiving the robe from the previous Patriarch, and had already decided it was time to stop. The flag remark gave him away before he said who he was.

---

**3/**
The monk lecturing that day, Yinzong, invited him to a seat of honor, pressed him with questions, and finally asked outright: "Aren't you the one who received the robe at Huangmei?" Huineng answered with a word of humility. He didn't claim it, but he didn't deny it either.

---

**4/**
Here's a correction worth making: most people assume the Platform Sutra recorded this scene first and the Gateless Gate (1228) picked it up later. It runs closer to the other way around.

---

**5/**
The earliest surviving Platform Sutra manuscript doesn't contain this scene at all. The earliest known telling is a separate 781 biography, with different wording and a different scene entirely: several monks debating four positions, not two men facing off.

---

**6/**
The Platform Sutra edition most people read today, with the familiar line, comes from a compilation finished in 1291, sixty-three years after the Gateless Gate. There's no way to confirm the exchange happened exactly as quoted, or as a face-off between two monks at all.

---

**7/**
Nobody was arguing about whether the flag was moving. That was happening directly in front of them, beyond dispute. The disagreement was only ever about where to place the cause. Huineng's answer stood outside that argument entirely.

---

**8/**
"The market did this." "No, execution did this." Same argument in different clothes. More often than either side admits, what's actually moving is the person unsettled by the number.

---

**9/**
Huineng wasn't pointing at the wind or the flag. He was pointing at the person watching.
