**1/**
A monk asks a teacher the biggest question there is: what is Buddha?

Tradition says the teacher was in the storeroom at the time, weighing out hemp for the monastery's rope and cloth. He doesn't reach for a sutra, a metaphor, or a moment of dramatic silence. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
He looks up and says: three pounds of flax.

---

**3/**
The teacher was Dongshan Shouchu (910–990), a student of the influential teacher Yunmen Wenyan. The exchange survives in two of Chan's major koan collections, which is unusual company for two lines about hemp.

---

**4/**
It's Case 12 of the Blue Cliff Record, compiled by Yuanwu Keqin in 1125 as commentary on an earlier verse collection by Xuedou Chongxian around 1038. It's also Case 18 of the Gateless Gate, which Wumen Huikai put together in 1228.

---

**5/**
Worth being precise here. Both base texts record the same two lines and nothing more: a monk asks what Buddha is, Dongshan answers three pounds of flax.

---

**6/**
Neither original case spells out the storeroom, the scale, or the hemp mid-weighing. That scene is the traditional context handed down alongside the exchange, repeated so consistently that it's effectively part of the story now. It isn't in the base text word for word.

---

**7/**
Here's a detail worth sitting with, even though the base text doesn't spell this out either: three catties of hemp was, by the standard of the time, roughly what it took to weave one monk's work robe.

---

**8/**
So when he was asked for the largest possible answer, Dongshan is said to have given, in effect, the raw material of the clothes on his own back. Not a symbol chosen for the occasion. Just the actual weight on the scale, which happened to equal a person's covering.

---

**9/**
It's also worth resisting the easy version of the takeaway. Yuanwu's own commentary on Case 12 is unusually blunt: plenty of students decide they've understood the exchange the moment they read it as "answer with whatever's in front of you".

---

**10/**
Treated as a formula, Yuanwu warns, that reading misses the point as thoroughly as reaching for grand abstraction would. Turning "stay concrete" into a technique is just a smaller kind of performance.

---

**11/**
The pull the koan is actually working on is familiar enough. Asked something enormous, we answer at the same scale, reaching for language that sounds proportionate to the size of the question.

---

**12/**
Dongshan's reply never rises to meet "what is Buddha" with anything resembling majesty. It stays exactly where he was standing, mid-task, in the plainest kind of storeroom labor.

---

**13/**
And per Yuanwu's warning, the lesson isn't "always answer this way as a rule." It's closer to: notice how much effort usually goes into sounding equal to the question, and ask what that effort is actually for.

---

**14/**
Consider the vision question. Asked where the team is headed, there's real pressure to supply something inspirational rather than the plainer, truer answer: here's what we're actually doing this week, and why.

---
