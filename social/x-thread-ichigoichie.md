---

**1/**
We schedule meetings as if they were reruns. "Let's circle back next week." "We'll pick this up next time."

There's a Japanese phrase that makes a quietly unsettling claim: there is no next time, not really. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Ichigo ichie (一期一会), "one lifetime, one meeting": this exact gathering, these people, this mood, this version of them and of you, assembles once and never again.

---

**3/**
The phrase comes out of the tea ceremony. Ichigo (一期) means one lifetime, birth to death. Ichie (一会), a single meeting.

---

**4/**
It was set down in the mid-1800s by the tea master and statesman Ii Naosuke, who was building on an earlier remark by a disciple of the great Sen no Rikyū: however many times host and guest might meet again, this gathering happens exactly once.

---

**5/**
Tea and Zen share a root here: cha-zen ichimi, "tea and Zen, one taste". That root is impermanence. Nothing recurs. The same tea, poured for the same guest tomorrow, is a different occasion entirely.

---

**6/**
Worth being precise about the history: the phrasing was refined over generations before Ii Naosuke fixed it in writing. What survives with certainty is the teaching, not a clean chain of single-author attribution.

---

**7/**
Most of daily life runs on the assumption of repetition. It's a useful assumption, but it quietly corrodes presence.

---

**8/**
If there's always a next time, this time doesn't fully count. So you show up at seventy percent, check your phone under the table, and save your real attention for a version of the meeting that never arrives.

---

**9/**
The trouble is that "next time" is a different meeting entirely. The person will have changed. The context will have moved on. Some of the people at the table won't be there at all.

---

**10/**
The meeting you half-attend today isn't deferred to a better one. It's simply spent, at seventy percent, and gone.

---

**11/**
Consider the introduction you rush. Meeting someone for the first time happens exactly once. Whatever attention you fail to bring, you don't get to bring later. First meetings have no retake.

---

**12/**
There's a version of this that has nothing to do with the office. We keep deferring presence to a calmer future: once this project ships, once things settle down, I'll really be there for the people who matter to me.

---

**13/**
But every ordinary dinner, every unremarkable phone call on the drive home, is a one-time gathering. The particular people at your table tonight will not all be there later.
