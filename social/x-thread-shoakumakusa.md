**1/**
Bai Juyi, the newly appointed governor of Hangzhou, went to see a monk who lived in a pine tree, sitting on a branch high enough that a fall would have killed him.

"Master, your position looks extremely dangerous."
"Governor, yours is far more dangerous than mine." 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Bai Juyi laughed him off — he governed the rivers and mountains of the region, what danger could there be in that?

Bird's Nest answered with an old pairing: firewood and flame tangle the instant they touch. The mind's habitual churning never stops.

---

**3/**
Bai Juyi pressed on: what's the great meaning of the Buddhist teaching?

Bird's Nest: "Do no evil, do all good."
Bai Juyi, one of the most celebrated poets of his age, laughed again: "Even a three-year-old knows that."
Bird's Nest didn't argue the point.

**4/**
"A three-year-old can say it. An eighty-year-old man still can't do it."

**5/**
The fuller version most people know comes from a source compiled around 1004. An earlier surviving source (c. 952) tells a plainer one: no governor, no tree-danger exchange, just a direct question about practice, and the retort aimed at a hundred-year-old man instead.

---

**6/**
The danger Bai Juyi could see (a monk on a branch) was obvious and irrelevant. The danger he couldn't see — appetite, status, the ordinary churn of wanting — was the one actually capable of doing damage. It felt too familiar to register as risk at all.

Most professional wisdom clears this same low bar. Give feedback while it's still useful instead of saving it for a review months later. Listen instead of drafting your reply while the other person is still talking.
