---

**1/**
Chest bare, feet bare, smeared with dirt and ash, grinning ear to ear, a man walks into a market town full of wine shops and fish stalls.

This is the tenth and final picture in one of Zen's most famous visual sequences — and it comes after complete realization, not before it. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
The sequence is the Ten Ox-Herding Pictures, most widely known in the version attributed to Kuoan Shiyuan. An earlier version ended at a single empty circle: the ox, and the seeker searching for it, both forgotten. Kuoan judged that ending incomplete.

---

**3/**
In Kuoan's ten-stage sequence, the circle is the eighth stage: nothing drawn, nothing left of the ox or the self chasing it. The ninth shows nature exactly as it is, with no person pictured anywhere. Only at the tenth does a human figure reappear.

---

**4/**
Not as someone transformed into something rarefied. He walks straight back into the noise of an ordinary market.

The verse everyone remembers: caked in dirt and ash, grinning ear to ear, no immortal's secret technique required — just a withered tree bursting back into flower.

---

**5/**
The empty circle looks, at a glance, like the obvious place to end: nothing left to want, nothing left to lose. Kuoan's version argues the opposite — that stopping there is its own quiet form of hiding.

---

**6/**
Two more stages were needed precisely because emptiness, held onto as an achievement, becomes one more thing to protect from the mess of ordinary contact.

---

**7/**
The instinct the tenth picture argues against shows up plainly at work. Someone spends years earning real expertise, then starts operating from a small distance above the people still doing the messy version of the job.

---

**8/**
Distance is just what accumulated competence tends to produce, quietly and mostly without any arrogance behind it, unless something pushes back against it.

The tenth picture pushes back on purpose. The mud and the ash aren't an accident the awakened man failed to avoid.

---

**9/**
They're the point — proof he actually went back.
