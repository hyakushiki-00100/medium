---

**1/**
Asked by a monk how to escape the cold and the heat when they arrive, a teacher gives what sounds like directions.

Go to the place, he says, where there's no cold and no heat. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Where is that place, the monk asks.

The teacher answers: when it's cold, let the cold kill you. When it's hot, let the heat kill you.

The first answer sounded like a granted request. The second refuses to point anywhere. What he offers isn't a destination.

---

**3/**
"Heat and cold" isn't about weather here. In Chan idiom it covers anything uncomfortable, the whole category of things worth escaping. Nothing's wrong with seeking warmth in winter and shade in summer. What the monk wants is a place where discomfort itself doesn't exist.

---

**4/**
The exchange survives as case forty-three of the Blue Cliff Record, compiled in 1125 by Yuanwu Keqin, built on verse commentary Xuedou Chongxian wrote roughly a century earlier. The teacher was Dongshan Liangjie (807–869), founder of what became the Sōtō school in Japan.

---

**5/**
The dialogue itself likely predates that verse, preserved first in Dongshan's own recorded sayings. So what survives isn't a transcript of one afternoon. It's a line that multiple generations of teachers judged worth keeping in circulation.

---

**6/**
"Let it kill you" sounds violent for a teaching about weather. It isn't pointing at anything close to literal death. What's asked for is total, unreserved absorption into whatever's actually happening: no leftover self standing outside it, waiting for it to pass.

---

**7/**
Telling a monk on a freezing stone floor to let the cold kill him isn't an instruction to grit his teeth and endure. It's an instruction to stop treating the shivering as a problem happening to someone separate from the shivering.

---

**8/**
Tight deadlines, a hard negotiation, work you haven't done before: a lot of people treat all of it as weather to get through until the next calm stretch. Then the next project arrives, carrying the same postponement. The cool place stays exactly one commitment ahead.

---

**9/**
Dongshan isn't telling anyone to take on less work. He's asking whether the cool place being searched for was ever actually out there.

---

**10/**
