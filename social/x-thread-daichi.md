---

**1/**
In front of the whole assembly, a teacher makes a claim: pick up the entire earth and it's the size of a single grain of rice.

Then he throws it down in front of them, right where they're sitting. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Nobody sees it.

---

**3/**
The teacher was Xuefeng Yicun (822–908), a disciple of Deshan Xuanjian and later teacher to a run of students unusually productive even by Chan standards, including Yunmen Wenyan, who went on to found his own school.

---

**4/**
"The whole great earth" is about as large an object as Chan rhetoric ever reaches for. Bigger than a mountain, bigger than a life's work. It's the largest available stand-in for anything that feels too vast to hold.

---

**5/**
Xuefeng's claim is that gathered into an actual hand, doing an actual thing, it shrinks to the size of a grain of rice.

The size was never the obstacle. The gathering was.

---

**6/**
What's actually recorded is worth reading exactly. The case is number five of the Blue Cliff Record, a 1125 collection by Yuanwu Keqin built on verses Xuedou Chongxian had written roughly a century earlier.

---

**7/**
Xuefeng's words in full: "Gather up the whole great earth in your hand and it's the size of a grain of rice. I throw it down right in front of you, and still, like lacquer buckets, you don't get it. Beat the drum — everyone, come out and look."

---

**8/**
Lacquer bucket was a stock insult in Chan teaching. It means pitch-black inside, blind to what's directly in view.

---

**9/**
So the students weren't missing something distant or subtle. It had already landed in front of them, and they still couldn't see it.

---

**10/**
Most retellings stop at the grain of rice and leave out what Xuefeng does next. He doesn't repeat the demonstration. He doesn't explain the metaphor. He calls for the drum.

---

**11/**
Consider the strategic problem no one will touch. "Fix our culture" or "solve retention" gets discussed at the scale of the whole organization, which is exactly the scale at which no one can do anything about it.

---

**12/**
The moment someone names one actual behavior in one actual meeting, the whole-organization problem becomes a grain of rice: something a hand can close around.

---

**13/**
His drum call is a single technical term from monastic life, and it changes what the case is asking for. The piece unpacks it, then finds two more places a problem gets held at a scale nobody can act on, plus the one you're holding at arm's length now 👇
