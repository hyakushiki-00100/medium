**1/**
"Don't rush the results — they'll come in time."

Most people hear that as a pep talk for the impatient.

Trace it to its Zen origin, though, and it's not encouragement. It's a description of how a plum tree is actually built. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Bodhidharma, the semi-legendary founder of Zen in China, is said to have left this verse for his student Huike shortly before his death, recorded at the end of the Shōshitsu Rokumon-shū.

---

**3/**
"I came originally to this land to transmit the teaching and save deluded beings. One flower opens five petals, and the fruit forms of itself."

This is the source of ikke goyō o hiraku (一華開五葉).

---

**4/**
The round, tumbling daruma doll everyone in Japan knows is modeled on this same Bodhidharma.

The toy is a lighthearted echo of a man whose last recorded words were about patience.

---

**5/**
Tradition holds two readings of this line side by side. Neither is treated as correct with the other dismissed as a mistake.

First: Bodhidharma was predicting his one school of Zen would branch into five and flourish.

---

**6/**
That prediction held up. Chinese Zen later split into five recognized lineages, including what became Rinzai and Sōtō.

---

**7/**
Second, associated with the teacher Dōgen: "one flower" and "five petals" aren't a prophecy about the future at all. They point to nothing more than the state of mind, fully present, right here and now. Flower and petals are already here.

---

**8/**
Whichever reading you take, the image underneath is about sequence.

Flower opens first. Leaves fill in. Only then does fruit form. This order can't be reversed, and it can't be skipped.

---

**9/**
Zen temples traditionally hang this verse as a scroll in early spring, during plum blossom season, when flowers push through before the cold has fully lifted, leaves following only afterward.

The sequence in the verse and the sequence on the branch line up exactly.

---

**10/**
We tend to try to reverse it anyway: results before the groundwork, recognition before trust, a harvest before the seed is even in the ground.

Preparing the soil is real work, not passivity. You just don't get to also decide when the fruit forms.

---

**11/**
One place this changes how a stalled project gets read at work: still in the flower stage, learning a new skill, building trust with a team? Don't demand fruit yet. Forcing a harvest that early tends to damage the flower itself.
