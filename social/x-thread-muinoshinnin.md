**1/**
On this lump of red flesh, a teacher tells his monks, there's a true person with no rank at all, constantly going in and out through the gates of your own face. Those of you who haven't seen it, look, look. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
A monk steps forward and asks: what is this person with no rank? The teacher comes down from his seat, grabs him, and demands: speak, speak. The monk hesitates. The teacher shoves him away, dismisses him with the crudest phrase he has on hand, and walks back to his room.

---

**3/**
The teacher was Linji Yixuan: birth year unrecorded, died in 866, founder of the school later known in Japan as Rinzai. A monastery, like most institutions, ran on rank: years of seniority, formal titles, how close you sat to the teacher.

---

**4/**
"No rank" is doing specific work here: not humble, not lowly, but outside the ladder of status. Linji's claim: the ranking system never touched it and couldn't confer it. It was already there, doing the ordinary work of seeing and hearing, before anyone assigned it a position.

---

**5/**
The record behind the exchange has its own layers. The Record of Linji credits its first collection to his disciple Sansheng Huiran, but the version that survives and circulates today didn't reach its settled form until 1120, edited into shape by Yuanjue Zongyan.

---

**6/**
That's roughly two and a half centuries after Linji died. What we're reading is a teaching the tradition kept sharpening, not a transcript taken in the room that day. That doesn't make it less real. It's the version later practitioners judged worth preserving exactly this way.

---

**7/**
The monk's question wasn't unreasonable, either. If there's a true person in here, show me.

Linji's answer wasn't an answer at all. He didn't describe it, define it, or point to a body part. He grabbed the man and demanded he speak first, on the spot, with nothing prepared.

---

**8/**
Watch the org chart do the weighing. An idea gets judged by whose mouth it came out of instead of whether it's right: junior analyst versus VP, same sentence, different reception.

---

**9/**
Structure still matters here. Meetings need someone running them, and someone has to actually decide. Linji's answer isn't against any of that. The rank is real and useful. It just was never the thing capable of the work.
