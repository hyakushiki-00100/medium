---

**1/**
"Just let it go."

It's the advice everyone gives and almost nobody can follow.

There's a Zen phrase behind that advice, and it asks for something harder: even your letting-go is one more thing you're still holding. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Zhaozhou (778–897) was a Tang-dynasty Zen master famous for answering enormous questions with almost nothing at all. He once asked two monks the same question, have you been here before. They gave opposite answers and got the same instruction back: go have some tea.

---

**3/**
One day a monk came to him and announced, with evident pride: "I have brought not a single thing." He meant it as an achievement: no attachments, no baggage, arrived empty-handed.

---

**4/**
Zhaozhou said: "Put it down." (Hōgejaku.)

The monk was baffled: "But I've brought nothing at all — what is there to put down?"

---

**5/**
Zhaozhou replied: "Then carry it off with you."

---

**6/**
Read quickly, it sounds like a riddle. But the monk had, in fact, brought something heavy: the idea "I have nothing." His emptiness had become a possession. His non-attachment had become the one thing he was most attached to.

---

**7/**
The character 著 on the end isn't decoration. It's an emphatic imperative: now, this instant, drop it. Not someday. Not as a project. Now.

---

**8/**
Worth being honest about the source: exchanges like this were preserved and shaped by later generations, collected in Zen anthologies compiled well after Zhaozhou's lifetime. Not a courtroom transcript. A teaching distilled by the tradition instead.

---

**9/**
Most of us know the obvious loads: the task list, the unread inbox, the worry replayed at 2 a.m. Hōgejaku points instead at the ones we're quietly proud of — the ones that never show up on a to-do list.

---

**10/**
One of them: the identity of the person who handles everything. Delegating a task is easy. Delegating the belief that only you can do it right is the real weight. Until that's set down, the task comes back within a week.

---

**11/**
There's a sharper, inward version too. The person who says "Oh, I've completely let that go" is very often the one carrying it hardest. Notice how much energy it takes just to keep insisting you've moved on.
