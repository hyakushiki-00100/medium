---

**1/**
In a Japanese house, the genkan is the small sunken space just inside the front door, where you step out of your shoes before stepping up into the home.

It's the most overlooked spot in the building. Its name means "the gate to the profound." 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Break the word apart. Gen (玄) means dark, mysterious, profound: the same character used for the deep and subtle Way. Kan (関) means a gate, a barrier, a checkpoint.

---

**3/**
Together, genkan originally named the entrance to the profound teaching itself: the threshold you crossed to enter the deep.

---

**4/**
In Zen temples the word came to mark the formal entrance to the abbot's quarters, the gate through which a seeker passed toward the teaching.

---

**5/**
From there it spread to samurai residences, then ordinary homes, into the tiled little entryway every Japanese house has today.

Worth flagging: the finer points of that history are debated, but the lineage from Buddhist term to front hall is the standard account.

---

**6/**
Here's what the humble genkan still quietly does. You stop. You step down. You lower yourself to take off your shoes. You step up onto a different floor level. In a few unremarkable seconds, your body performs a complete crossing.

---

**7/**
A threshold, in other words, is a device for changing state. Not a gap between two rooms, but the mechanism that makes them two rooms at all.

---

**8/**
Cultures have always sensed this. We ring bells at temple gates, carry brides over doorways, cut ribbons to open buildings. The ceremony collects at the threshold because the threshold is where the change actually happens.

---

**9/**
Modern life has been quietly demolishing its thresholds. Work and home dissolve into the same laptop. One meeting bleeds into the next with no gate between them.

---

**10/**
No genkan, so no change of state. We track each room's mud into the next.

---

**11/**
One place to rebuild it: before deep work, build a thirty-second gate. Close the tabs, and cross deliberately, the way you'd step out of your shoes. The crossing isn't lost time. It's what makes the next hour a different room.

---

**12/**
There's a quieter point hiding in the etymology too. We tend to imagine depth lies behind some special, distant door. But genkan puts the gate to the profound at the most ordinary spot in the house: the entrance.
