**1/**
The Buddha compared his teaching to rain.

One cloud. Same rain. Falls equally on every plant — grass, shrub, giant tree.

Yet each one grows to a completely different height.

Was the rain unfair? Or were we asking the wrong question about "equal" all along? 🧵👇

---

**2/**
This is the Parable of the Medicinal Herbs, Chapter 5 of the Lotus Sutra.

The Buddha tells his disciple Mahakashyapa: picture mountains, valleys, plains covered in grasses, shrubs, and trees of every size.

One cloud spreads across the sky and rains on all of it.

---

**3/**
Each small herb draws up water in proportion to its own roots and stem.
Each great tree draws up water in proportion to its own trunk and branches.

Same rain. Different absorption. Different growth — each according to its own capacity.

---

**4/**
What's striking: the parable doesn't treat this difference as a flaw.

A small herb growing less than a great tree isn't evidence the rain was unfair. It simply has less root to draw water with.

The teaching makes no attempt to flatten that difference.

---

**5/**
In the parable, "equal" quietly means something specific:

Equal ≠ guaranteeing the same outcome for everyone
Equal = offering the same opportunity (the rain) to everyone

Different growth speeds aren't a failure. They're what different capacity naturally produces.

---

**6/**
Two places this lands at work:

Same training, different speeds — not unfair, just different prior experience, the "vessel" each person brings.

Identical feedback changes some behavior and not others'. Worth checking readiness before rewriting the feedback.

---

**7/**
A manager pushes to "develop everyone the same way" and grows anxious at every gap in growth rate.

But providing equal rain — equal access, equal information, equal support — and shaping everyone into an identical form are simply two different jobs.

---

**8/**
What a coach or manager can actually control ends at providing the rain, consistently and equally.

What grows from it — and how much — is left to the recipient's own capacity and time.

Holding that line clearly can spare a lot of needless self-blame.

---

**9/**
One question worth asking: am I confusing "provide the rain equally" with "control exactly how every tree grows"?

There's a second one that goes with it — about what to check before treating a growth gap as proof coaching failed.

---

**10/**
Not a call to lower the bar. Just a distinction worth holding onto.
