元URL(CTA用): https://medium.com/@mk3372/a-monk-asked-if-a-dog-has-buddha-nature-the-teacher-said-no-which-should-have-been-41780a1ae5bf

---

**1/**
A monk puts a doctrinal question to an old teacher: does a dog have Buddha-nature?

By every text the monk had studied, the answer was settled before he opened his mouth. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
The teacher answers anyway: "Mu." No.

No argument, no scripture, no softening. The exchange ends there.

---

**3/**
The doctrine comes from the Nirvana Sutra, which states plainly that all sentient beings possess Buddha-nature. Asking about a dog was a formality, and Zhaozhou Congshen (778–897) was expected to say yes. "Mu" was an answer that shouldn't have been available at all.

---

**4/**
There isn't much primary source to read here, which is itself the point. Case 1 of the Mumonkan, which Wumen Huikai assembled in 1228, reads in full: "A monk asked Zhaozhou, 'Does a dog have Buddha-nature or not?' Zhaozhou said, 'Mu.'" No scene, no reasoning attached.

---

**5/**
Later retellings that give the exchange a fuller shape are additions the base text doesn't support. It isn't the only version on record either. Hongzhi Zhengjue's Extensive Record, compiled decades before Wumen's case, preserves the same "no" but doesn't leave it standing alone.

---

**6/**
There a monk asks the same question, gets the same answer, then presses: every sentient being is said to have Buddha-nature, so why not this one? Zhaozhou answers, "because it carries karmic consciousness." A reason existed. The version that became canonical strips it out.

---

**7/**
Wumen's own commentary, printed in the same 1228 text as the case, is unusually direct about what not to do with that word. "Day and night, take it up," he writes. "Don't understand it as blank nothingness. Don't understand it in terms of existence or non-existence."

---

**8/**
He compares it to having already swallowed a red-hot iron ball: however hard you try, it won't come back up. The instruction isn't to solve "Mu" as a riddle. It's to hold the actual discomfort of not having an answer, rather than resolve it early with a clever guess.

---

**9/**
Status meetings run on this. Someone asks a question nobody in the room has enough information to answer, and someone answers anyway, because silence reads as incompetence and a wrong-but-confident guess doesn't. Zhaozhou wasn't managing anyone's discomfort, including his own.

---

**10/**
