# X thread — 香厳撃竹 (Xiangyan Hits Bamboo) — 元記事: articles/medium/kyogen-en.md

---

**1/**
Xiangyan Zhixian was, by every account, the most well-read monk in the monastery: the one who could answer any question about scripture on the spot.

Then his teacher asked him something no book could answer. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Xiangyan trained under Guishan Lingyou (771–853), one of the major Chan teachers of the mid-Tang period. One day Guishan put a single question to him: "What was your original face, before your parents were born?"

---

**3/**
It wasn't a doctrinal question. Nothing Xiangyan's considerable learning had prepared him for. He searched his memory, his books, every sutra he'd ever studied, and came up with nothing that satisfied even himself.

---

**4/**
He asked Guishan directly for the answer. Guishan refused: "If I told you, you'd curse me for it later — and whatever I said would be mine, not yours."

---

**5/**
Xiangyan went back to his room, pulled every text he owned, and burned the lot. "A painted rice cake doesn't satisfy hunger," he reportedly said.

---

**6/**
He gave up scholarship entirely and settled at the ruins associated with National Teacher Nanyang Huizhong, clearing brush and weeds around the site for years.

---

**7/**
The turn came on an ordinary afternoon of that ordinary work. A chip of tile, swept up along with the brush, struck a stalk of bamboo and cracked. At the sound, something that all his reading had never produced arrived at once.

---

**8/**
Worth being honest about the record: this is preserved in the Jingde Record of the Transmission of the Lamp, compiled in 1004, roughly a century and a half after Guishan's death. The precise wording is the tradition's distillation, not a witnessed transcript.

---

**9/**
What's worth sitting with is what actually failed him. Xiangyan wasn't stupid, and he wasn't lazy. He was exactly the kind of person modern life rewards most: fluent, well-read, quick with an answer.

---

**10/**
None of that was disqualifying. But it had become a substitute. He could describe the taste of the rice cake in exquisite technical detail without ever having to admit he'd never actually eaten one.

---

**11/**
Take the framework that replaces the decision. Citing the right model, the right precedent, the right authority can function as a way of never actually committing to a judgment of your own.

---

**12/**
Xiangyan's scholarship was never the problem, and Guishan never told him to stay ignorant. Burning the books wasn't the point. Only what burning them made room to notice.

---

**13/**
There's a version of Guishan's question every person eventually meets: not the exact words, necessarily, but something in that shape. No accumulated cleverness answers it.

---
