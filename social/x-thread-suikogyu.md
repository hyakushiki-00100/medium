**1/**
In a hundred years, an old teacher tells his students, after I've left this world, I'll be reborn as a water buffalo at the foot of this mountain.

On my left side will be written five characters: this monk of Guishan. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
If you call me "the monk of Guishan" at that point, I'm also a water buffalo. If you call me "water buffalo," I'm also the monk of Guishan.

So what would be the right thing to call me?

No one answered.

---

**3/**
Long before that, Guishan Lingyou (771–853) trained under Baizhang Huaihai. Baizhang set a pitcher before the assembly: you may not call this a pitcher, so what do you call it? Hualin, the senior disciple, went first. "You can't call it a block of wood either," he offered.

---

**4/**
Guishan said nothing. He walked over, kicked the pitcher onto its side, and left the room.

Baizhang laughed. "Hualin lost to a pitcher," he said. "Guishan is the one who'll run the mountain."

---

**5/**
The pitcher story is recorded in the Song Biographies of Eminent Monks and in the Jingde Chuandenglu, compiled in 1004. The water buffalo story survives separately, in Guishan's own recorded sayings. What connects them is that both are about names failing to hold still.

---

**6/**
Exactly when that collection was compiled isn't something that can be confirmed. But Guishan's life and teaching are independently documented in the 1004 record, so there's no doubt he was a real historical figure whose words were being preserved.

---

**7/**
In the first story, Guishan answered a ban on naming with an action instead of a word. In the second, decades later, he handed his students a case where two contradictory names could both be true of the same thing at once. As far as the record shows, nobody had an answer ready.

---

**8/**
Picture the buffalo: pulling a plow, five characters on its flank that nobody working alongside it has any reason to notice. A farmer who saw only the animal would use it like any other buffalo. Someone who read those characters wouldn't know if it was livestock or a teacher.

---

**9/**
The discomfort Guishan built into the question: one creature can honestly answer to two names that appear to rule each other out. Sales calls it a pricing problem; engineering calls it a quality problem. Minutes spent arguing over the label aren't spent on what both point at.

---

**10/**
Guishan wasn't asking anyone to find the correct name. What he was asking for instead, two more meetings that stall on a label, and the two contradictory names you answer to depending on which room you're in: all of that starts where this thread stops 👇
