**1/**
In a hundred years, an old teacher tells his students, after I've left this world, I'll be reborn as a water buffalo at the foot of this mountain.

On my left side will be written five characters: this monk of Guishan. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
If you call me "the monk of Guishan" at that point, I'm also a water buffalo. If you call me "water buffalo," I'm also the monk of Guishan.

So what would be the right thing to call me?

---

**3/**
No one answered.

---

**4/**
Long before he said any of that, Guishan Lingyou (771–853) was a student under Baizhang Huaihai, not yet a teacher of anyone.

---

**5/**
One day Baizhang set a water pitcher in front of the assembly and posed a challenge: you may not call this a pitcher, so what do you call it?

---

**6/**
Hualin, the senior disciple, stepped forward first. "You can't call it a block of wood either," he offered.

---

**7/**
Guishan said nothing. He walked over, kicked the pitcher onto its side, and left the room.

---

**8/**
Baizhang laughed. "Hualin lost to a pitcher," he said. "Guishan is the one who'll run the mountain."

That's how Guishan came to found a monastery on the mountain that would carry his name for the rest of Chan history.

---

**9/**
The pitcher story is recorded in the Song Biographies of Eminent Monks and in the Jingde Chuandenglu, compiled in 1004. The water buffalo story survives separately, in Guishan's own recorded sayings.

---

**10/**
Exactly when that collection was compiled isn't something that can be confirmed. But Guishan's life and teaching are independently documented in the 1004 record, so there's no doubt he was a real historical figure whose words were being preserved.

---

**11/**
What connects the two stories is that both are about names failing to hold still. In the first, Guishan answered a ban on naming with an action instead of a word.

---

**12/**
In the second, decades later as an old teacher, he handed his own students a case where two contradictory names could both be true of the same thing at once. And as far as the record shows, nobody in the room had an answer ready.

---

**13/**
Picture the buffalo itself. Pulling a plow, hauling loads, sweating through a field somewhere at the base of the mountain, five characters marked on its flank that nobody working alongside it has any reason to notice.

---

**14/**
A farmer who saw only the animal would use it like any other buffalo: the whip, the yoke, a full day's labor. Someone who happened to read those five characters would suddenly be unsure whether they were looking at livestock or at a teacher who deserved respect.

---

**15/**
That's the discomfort Guishan built into the question. Not "which name is correct," but the fact that a single, actual creature can honestly answer to two names that appear to rule each other out.

---

**16/**
Sit in the meeting where the same issue gets two names. Sales calls it a pricing problem. Engineering calls it a quality problem. Every minute spent arguing over which label is correct is a minute not spent on the thing both labels are pointing at.

---

**17/**
Guishan wasn't asking anyone to find the correct name. What he was asking for instead, two more meetings that stall on a label, and the two contradictory names you answer to depending on which room you're in: all of that starts where this thread stops 👇
