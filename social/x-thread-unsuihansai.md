---

**1/**
Layman Pang came from a family with an open road into government office.

After a turn in his practice, he loaded the entire family fortune onto a boat, rowed to the middle of the river, and sank it. Not donated. Not distributed. Sunk. 🧵👇

---

**2/**
He was born into a family of officials in Hengyang — his own father is said to have governed the region. In his mid-forties he went to study with Shitou Xiqian, who asked him a plain question: since arriving here, what have your daily activities actually been?

---

**3/**
Pang answered with a verse. The closing couplet is the part that survived:

"Supernatural power, marvelous action — carrying water, chopping wood."

He spent the rest of his life selling bamboo goods for a living. He never became a monk. No robes, no ordination, no office.

---

**4/**
One earlier line in that verse is doing real work, not decoration: "Who decided which colors mark the rank of officials?"

Purple and vermilion were the literal, legally prescribed dress of high Tang officials. For a man from Pang's background, that color was a live option.

---

**5/**
Asked why he hadn't given the fortune to the poor instead, he's said to have answered that it had been a source of harm to him for a long time, and he had no wish to pass that harm on to anyone else.

That's the reason the tradition kept.

---

**6/**
The habit Pang refused shows up today in a narrower shape: needing good work to leave a trail. Catching a problem quietly before it becomes anyone else's, then making sure it's known who caught it. Slipping the unglamorous fix into the next update, just so it registers.

---

**7/**
None of that is dishonest — sometimes the update is genuinely useful information. The real distinction is narrower: whether the report exists to inform people, or exists because the work didn't feel complete until it had a witness.

---

**8/**
Pang kept the sutras he'd studied and let go of everything that would have made his practice legible to anyone watching. What remained was a man hauling water and splitting firewood, on a day exactly like every other, with nothing left to point to as proof it had mattered.
