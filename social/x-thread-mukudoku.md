---

**1/**
Emperor Wu of Liang had, by any reasonable accounting, done an enormous amount of good. Temples built, monks funded, sutras copied, images cast.

So when Bodhidharma arrived at his court, the emperor asked a fair question: how much merit had all of that earned him? 🧵👇

---

**2/**
Bodhidharma's answer was two words: Mukudoku. No merit at all.

---

**3/**
Worth being upfront: Bodhidharma is traditionally credited as the founder of Zen in China, but how much of his biography is history and how much is later legend is genuinely unclear. His own dates are disputed, and this exchange survives mainly through much later retellings.

---

**4/**
Emperor Wu's own devotion, by contrast, is well documented and was extravagant even by the standards of pious rulers: on four separate occasions he donated himself to Tongtai Temple as an ordinary monk.

---

**5/**
Each time, his officials had to buy him back from monastic life with a small fortune in coin (once reportedly a hundred million, later two hundred million) before he could return to the throne. This was not a man making a modest inquiry about his standing.

---

**6/**
What isn't in dispute is how the story functions in the tradition. Emperor Wu lists his accomplishments and asks for the tally. Bodhidharma refuses to give him one.

---

**7/**
The emperor, baffled, presses further: asks what the highest truth of Buddhism actually is. Bodhidharma answers, "Vast emptiness, nothing holy."

---

**8/**
It's tempting to read this as Bodhidharma dismissing the emperor's works as worthless. He wasn't. The temples stayed built. The monks stayed fed. The refusal wasn't aimed at the deeds. It was aimed at the ledger the emperor was keeping alongside them.

---

**9/**
The deed itself doesn't stop counting because someone was hoping for a return on it. What changes is what's actually occupying the person at the moment of giving: part of their attention is on the gift, part is already at the teller's window, waiting for the payout.

---

**10/**
One example: the favor with an invoice attached. Help extended with half an eye on the future ask isn't generosity; it's financing. Resentment is what a loan looks like when it isn't repaid.

---

**11/**
None of this is an argument for self-erasure, or for pretending you don't want to be seen doing good work. Wanting to be seen is human. It's a distinction between the deed and the accounting attached to it.

---
