# His Answer Was the Wealth He Said He Lacked. The Zen Case "Seizei Kohin" (清税孤貧).

A monk named Qingshui told Caoshan¹, "I'm destitute. Please help me." Caoshan called back, "Reverend Qingshui." "Yes?" Qingshui answered, at once. Caoshan said, "You've already had three cups of the Bai family's wine from Quanzhou, and you're telling me your lips are still dry."

## A complaint answered before it was finished

Qingshui didn't get a lecture about wealth or poverty, and he didn't get turned away. He got called by his own name, and he answered — immediately, completely, without checking first whether he felt ready or worthy. That response was the whole transaction. Nothing about it required preparation, credentials, or a rehearsed opening line. Caoshan wasn't being coy about serving the wine later. He was pointing at the cup Qingshui had just finished, the one Qingshui had emptied without noticing he'd been handed it.

## Not a request for money

"I'm destitute, help me" is a familiar opening in this kind of exchange, and it isn't really a request for money. It's a request for instruction — for something to fill a gap the speaker believed was there. The gap was the whole premise. Caoshan isn't arguing that no one is ever genuinely lacking something. He's pointing at this one claim in particular, and at the proof of lack that should have shown up alongside it, a hesitant, uncertain response, and never did. What showed up instead, seconds earlier, was full presence, answering to his own name and title.

## The instant answer was a later touch-up

Most people encounter this exchange as Case 10 of the *Gateless Gate*² (無門関), compiled in 1228, the telling repeated here. It isn't the earliest one. The exchange already appears in Caoshan's own biography in the *Transmission of the Lamp*³ (景徳伝灯録), compiled in 1004, two centuries earlier, and there it reads slightly differently: told to step closer, the monk simply steps closer. The sharper version, the instant, wordless "yes" that most retellings now lead with, shows up first in Xuedou Chongxian's⁴ verse commentary, written sometime before his death in 1052, and from there hardens into the standard telling by the time the *Gateless Gate* was compiled. The proof got more dramatic each time someone retold it.

## A poverty with too much spirit in it

Wumen's verse on this case runs four lines: poor as Fan Dan⁵, spirited as Xiang Yu⁶ — and then, though he has no way to make a living, bold enough to compete at being rich. Fan Dan was a byword for scraping by without complaint; Xiang Yu was a general remembered for a fierceness that refused to bend even at his final defeat. Read one way, the pairing is gently mocking: a complaint about lack, delivered with the bearing of someone who has never once doubted his own standing enough to actually sound poor. Read the other way, it's closer to admiration — daring to claim wealth you can't show anyone is still a kind of wealth. Wumen doesn't settle it for you. Both readings agree on the same underlying fact: whatever Qingshui actually had, it wasn't the thing his opening line described.

## The shortage and the evidence against it arrive together

Teams say "we don't have the resources for this" while already running the thing they claim they can't afford to run: covering for an open role, shipping on the old budget, absorbing the extra client without asking for more headcount. The shortage they're naming and the capacity they're demonstrating arrive in the same breath, and almost nobody catches the second half. The complaint gets repeated in every planning meeting long after the workaround has quietly become the normal way the team operates.

## The habit gets sneakier without an audience

Nearly everyone keeps a running account of what they haven't earned yet — the confidence they're still waiting to feel, the competence they assume real experts have and they don't. Meanwhile the actual evidence, the steady, immediate, unhesitating way they already handle most of what's in front of them, gets filed separately, as if it doesn't count toward the total. A difficult conversation handled calmly this morning, a decision made without agonizing over it, a friend's crisis met with the right words on the first try — none of it gets credited toward the account it's actually paying into. It counts. It was never a separate ledger to begin with.

## In closing

Qingshui asked to be given something, and the giving had already happened before he finished the sentence. Caoshan didn't correct his math. He just refused to pretend the cup was still empty when it so obviously wasn't. Most complaints about not having enough survive on the same arithmetic error — counting only the wanting, and forgetting to count the wine that's already been drunk.
