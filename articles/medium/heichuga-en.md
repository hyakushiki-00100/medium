# A Man Asked How to Free a Goose Without Breaking Its Bottle. The Teacher Called Out His Official Title. The Zen Koan "Heichuga" (瓶中鵝).

Lu Gen¹, a government official, put a classic riddle to Nanquan²: someone raised a goose inside a bottle, and it grew too large to fit back through the neck. Without breaking the bottle and without harming the goose, how would you free it? Nanquan didn't pause over it. He called out at once: "Governor!" Lu Gen answered, "Yes?" Nanquan said: "There — it's out."

## What answered wasn't the governor

Nanquan didn't address Lu Gen by name. He used his title — the official rank Lu Gen had carried into the room along with the riddle, the identity that comes with a staff and an entourage and a reputation for asking sharp questions. Whatever was supposed to answer to that title would have had time to consider whether responding fit the dignity of the office, whether the riddle deserved a clever comeback befitting a governor. Nothing like that happened. Something in Lu Gen answered before any of it could be arranged, and it wasn't the governor who answered. It was whatever's left when a person hasn't yet decided how to perform being addressed. That's what had been outside the bottle the entire time the riddle was being turned over in his mind — not a version of Lu Gen clever enough to solve the puzzle, but the part of him too immediate to need to.

## An old man who'd been left alone for thirty years

Nanquan³ had spent roughly three decades in seclusion on his mountain before officials in the region, Lu Gen among them, petitioned him to come down and teach; by the time this exchange is generally placed, in the early Taihe years of the 830s, he'd only recently agreed. The record notes this was the first question Lu Gen ever put to him — not a test after years of familiarity, but an opening move from someone still finding out what kind of teacher he'd recruited. The clearest surviving account of the exchange is the Jingde-era record of the lamp⁴, compiled in 1004. Lu Gen has his own separate chapter in the earlier Anthology of the Patriarchal Hall⁵, compiled in 952, which preserves other exchanges between the two men, though this particular story doesn't turn up there.

## A knot handled a similar way, a thousand years earlier

More than a millennium before this exchange, in Phrygia, a wagon was said to be bound to its post by a knot so elaborate that no one could find either end of the rope, with a prophecy attached: whoever undid it would rule all of Asia. In the version most often repeated, Alexander looked at it once, drew his sword, and cut straight through — though at least one ancient source claims he simply pulled out a pin and slid the yoke free, solving it inside the knot's own terms rather than refusing them. Either way, what makes the story worth telling is the same thing: the knot was only impossible under the assumption that undoing it had to look a certain way.

## Three walls, arriving as one mandate

A team gets handed a mandate that sounds, on inspection, a lot like the goose: grow the number without adding headcount, without changing the product, without touching the price. Weeks get spent hunting for the clever configuration that satisfies all three at once, because the constraints arrived bundled together and it's easy to assume they have to be solved as a set. Sometimes they do. Often the more useful move is checking which of the three walls was ever actually load-bearing, instead of continuing to measure a goose that was never going to fit through any of the openings it was offered.

## The role, and what answers underneath it

People spend years practicing a role the same way Lu Gen wore his title — the leader who's always composed, the parent with an answer ready for everything, the friend who never seems rattled. Maintaining the performance takes real effort, and most days there's time to arrange it before anyone notices the gap. Then something ordinary happens too fast for the performance to load — a door slams, a child falls, a phone rings at the wrong hour — and whatever responds in that half-second is plainer and steadier than months of careful management of the role ever managed to be. The role wasn't wrong to build. It just was never the thing that actually answers.

## In closing

Lu Gen came with a riddle built to have no exit, and Nanquan didn't find one inside it. He addressed the office Lu Gen carried into the room, and something underneath the office answered before it had time to decide whether a governor should. That was already outside the bottle. It always had been.
